Corporate Tax

UAE Corporate Tax in 2026: what every business must know

By the Najma Al Raeda tax team12 June 20266 min read

UAE Corporate Tax applies to financial years beginning on or after 1 June 2023, and by 2026 it is simply part of doing business in the Emirates. The structure is deliberately competitive, but the compliance obligations are real — and the penalties for getting registration or filing wrong are not trivial.

How the rate actually works

Corporate Tax is charged on taxable income, not revenue. The first AED 375,000 of taxable income is taxed at 0%; anything above that is taxed at 9%. For most small and medium businesses that means a genuinely modest effective rate — a company with AED 600,000 of taxable income pays 9% on AED 225,000, an effective rate of just over 3%.

Registration is mandatory

Almost every business and “taxable person” in the UAE must register for Corporate Tax and obtain a Tax Registration Number, even if they expect to fall within the 0% band or qualify for relief. The FTA has set registration deadlines linked to the month your licence was issued, and late registration carries an administrative penalty. If you have not yet registered, treat it as urgent.

Free zone businesses: the 0% is conditional

A “Qualifying Free Zone Person” can continue to benefit from a 0% rate on qualifying income — but only if strict conditions are met, including maintaining adequate substance in the UAE, earning qualifying income, meeting transfer-pricing rules and not electing to be taxed at the standard rate. Non-qualifying income is taxed at 9%. Free zone status is not an automatic exemption; it must be earned and evidenced every year.

What you need in place

  • A Corporate Tax registration and Tax Registration Number.
  • Books of account kept to acceptable accounting standards (IFRS), retained for at least seven years.
  • A view on whether Small Business Relief or free-zone qualifying status applies to you.
  • Transfer-pricing documentation if you transact with related parties.
  • A filing calendar — the return is generally due within nine months of your financial year-end.

Common mistake we fix: businesses assume that because they expect to owe little or no tax, they do not need to register or file. The obligation to register and file exists regardless of how much tax is due — and the penalties are for non-compliance, not for the tax itself.

The bottom line

UAE Corporate Tax rewards businesses that keep clean books and plan ahead. The combination of the 0% band, Small Business Relief and free-zone qualifying income means many SMEs pay very little — provided they register on time, file correctly and can evidence their position. That is exactly the work we take off your plate.
