UAE VAT essentials: registration, filing and staying penalty-free

By the Najma Al Raeda tax team3 June 20265 min read

Value Added Tax has been part of UAE business life since 1 January 2018, yet VAT penalties remain the single most common avoidable cost we see in new client books. The rules are not complicated — but they are unforgiving of late filings and sloppy records.

Do you have to register?

Registration is mandatory once your taxable supplies and imports exceed AED 375,000 in the previous 12 months, or are expected to in the next 30 days. You may register voluntarily once you pass AED 187,500 — often worthwhile for B2B businesses that want to reclaim input VAT.

Filing and payment

Once registered, you file periodic VAT returns (usually quarterly, sometimes monthly) through the FTA portal, reporting output VAT charged and input VAT reclaimed. The return and payment are due by the 28th day after the end of the tax period. Miss it and penalties begin immediately.

Where businesses go wrong

  • Late filing or payment — the most common and most avoidable penalty.
  • Reclaiming input VAT without valid tax invoices — the FTA expects compliant documentation for every claim.
  • Mis-treating zero-rated vs exempt supplies — exports, certain healthcare and education are treated very differently, and getting it wrong distorts your return.
  • Ignoring the reverse-charge mechanism on imported services and goods.

Our standard: zero penalties. We track every client’s VAT period, prepare the return from reconciled books, and file well before the deadline — so a missed filing is simply not something our clients experience.

Keeping it clean

Good VAT compliance is really a by-product of good bookkeeping. When your records are reconciled monthly and your tax invoices are in order, the quarterly return becomes a formality rather than a scramble. That is the rhythm we build for every client.

This article is general guidance based on UAE Federal Tax Authority rules current at the time of writing and does not constitute formal tax advice. Rules, thresholds and deadlines change — always confirm the current position with the FTA or speak to our team before acting.
